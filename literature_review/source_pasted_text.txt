I treated “this bucket” as: **LLM/agent systems that generate, screen, evolve, backtest, or execute alpha factors/trading strategies**, where the paper/repo gives at least a plausible path to **positive risk-adjusted return / Sharpe / RankIC / alpha**. This does **not** mean I believe the reported Sharpe is live-tradable. Many of these results are backtest-only and vulnerable to data leakage, short-window overfitting, transaction-cost assumptions, and LLM pretraining contamination.

As of **July 4, 2026**, the public landscape breaks into three groups.

## My ranking for actual research use

**Tier 1 — start here for alpha-factor discovery:** AlphaBench, AlphaAgent, QuantaAlpha, QuantEvolver, R&D-Agent-Quant, EFS, Alpha-R1, AlphaForgeBench.

**Tier 2 — promising but needs code or stronger replication:** Alpha-Jungle, FactorMiner, CogAlpha, AlphaAgentEvo, AlphaCrafter, Chain-of-Alpha, FAMA, Alpha-GPT, FactorMAD, AlphaLogics.

**Tier 3 — direct trading agents, useful but more fragile:** TradingAgents, ContestTrade, AlphaQuanter, QuantAgent-HFT, FinMem, FinCon, FinAgent, FLAG-Trader, MM-DREX, Trading-R1, Janus-Q, MarketSenseAI, GuruAgents, HedgeAgents, QuantAgents.

**Tier 4 — evaluation / due diligence tools:** LiveTradeBench, DeepFund, AI-Trader, QuantCode-Bench, TradeTrap, AgenticTrading.

The core practical lesson is that **factor-generation frameworks are more credible than end-to-end “LLM decides trades” agents**. AlphaForgeBench explicitly argues for using LLMs as quant researchers generating executable alpha factors rather than as unstable sequential execution agents, and both DeepFund and AI-Trader show that live-style evaluation can sharply reduce or reverse the optimistic picture from ordinary historical backtests. ([arXiv][45])

I would not trust any reported Sharpe until it survives point-in-time data, realistic slippage/fees/borrow assumptions, purged walk-forward validation, deflated Sharpe or multiple-testing correction, subperiod analysis, capacity checks, and live or paper-trading confirmation. A 2026 critique of LLM trading-agent alpha specifically warns that many papers report high Sharpe or cumulative returns over short windows where researcher degrees of freedom and repeated search can inflate false positives. ([arXiv][55])
