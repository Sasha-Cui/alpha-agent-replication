# M058: Macro Economists in the Machine

Status: **closed not evaluable on monthly U.S./JKP data**.

The paper's central strategy is a weekly allocation across 15 commodity-related ETFs. Three specialist LLM macroeconomists consume prices, release-aware macro data, and narratives; debate disagreements; and emit probability contracts that tilt a 50/50 equal-weight and inverse-volatility base before cyclical, single-name, and turnover controls. This is a portfolio policy over ETFs, not a score formula over individual stocks.

The paper is equation-rich: 22 independently implemented mechanics pass controlled checks, including rule tilts, conflict attenuation, debate averaging, base allocation, multiplicative tilts, normalization, turnover, costs, and metrics. Four core operations remain fail-closed, including regime probabilities and constrained-weight projection. More importantly, the public source contains no implementation or artifact link; fresh exact repository searches remain empty, while processed data, cached contracts, and replication materials are offered only upon request.

The missing inputs are causal: Yahoo/FRED vintages, weekly dates, complete prompts/schema, model requests and responses, narratives, probability contracts, debate messages, the cyclical set, risk-control ordering, dated weights, trades, and returns. Controlled equations cannot generate weights without them. Applying the rule-loading matrix directly would remove the LLM/debate mechanism, and the 50/50 base is an input/baseline rather than the headline system.

There is also no paper-defined map from a 15-ETF commodity portfolio to monthly individual U.S. stocks or JKP characteristics. Inventing one would change the asset universe and strategy. No return path is fabricated; none of 132 table cells or 12 empirical panels was regenerated. This closure does not establish that the reported results are false, although conflicting transaction-cost figures and tables further prevent printed aggregates from replacing the absent raw paths.
