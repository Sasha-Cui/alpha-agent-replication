\begin{abstract}
Financial LLM agents often produce strategies that look profitable, but apparent profitability is not the same as alpha incremental to a broad factor benchmark. We screen 98 distinct works, retain 69 formula-discovery or trading papers, and construct 50 good-faith U.S. strategy reconstructions for all 40 retained works with enough public content for a component or motif mapping; the other 29 remain availability-only. Holding returns, costs, and evaluation months fixed, median annualized one-month-ahead residual return falls from 9.97\% under CAPM to 3.17\% under FF3, 2.69\% under FF5 plus momentum, and $-1.69\%$ under the market plus 132 Jensen--Kelly--Pedersen (JKP) factors; Holm-adjusted positive counts are 6, 1, 2, and 0 out of 50. Separately, we execute all 190 public GuruAgents prompt cells with GPT-4o through OpenRouter and form 24 costed replay paths. On the 12 paths with a common 33-month factor window, adding JKP betting-against-beta (BAB) to official FF5 plus momentum reduces median annualized alpha from 5.80\% to 2.59\% and attenuates 11 paths, but one Buffett replay remains Holm-positive. An exploratory all-JKP ridge test leaves no Holm-positive replay; unrestricted JKP132 OLS is unidentified in this short sample. Thus the proxy study shows broad attenuation, while the prompt replay demonstrates both that the LLM decision layer matters and that short-window attribution does not justify a universal absorption claim. The estimates are retrospective descriptive spanning diagnostics, not confirmatory tests of native-agent performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM-agent papers increasingly report profitable factors, superior trading performance, or investment alpha \cite{CensusACL2024findingsacl233,CensusArxiv250216789,CensusACL2025emnlpdemos14,CensusArxiv260515412,CensusDOI10114537682923770377}. Many of those strategies may indeed earn positive returns relative to the papers' chosen baselines. The central empirical question, however, is whether they add return information beyond familiar asset-pricing factors. Positive returns can coexist with exposures to low-risk, momentum, quality, or valuation factors, so separating incremental return information from familiar exposure requires an explicit spanning analysis.

Our evidence separates these claims. Across 50 reconstructions from 40 retained papers, apparent alpha remains positive against CAPM, FF3, and FF5 plus momentum, but collapses when the benchmark expands to the market plus 132 JKP characteristic factors \cite{JensenKellyPedersen2023}. Median annualized residual return moves from 9.97\% under CAPM to $-1.69\%$ under the broad model, and the Holm-adjusted discovery count falls from six to zero. The return series themselves point to the same explanation: 47 of 50 have an absolute correlation of at least 0.50 with their nearest JKP factor, and the median nearest-factor correlation is 0.81.

A direct replay adds a distinct test of the concern that a deterministic characteristic proxy may remove the mechanism of interest. For GuruAgents \cite{CensusArxiv251001664}, we rerun the released prompts and deterministic finance-tool observations through the paper's stated GPT-4o decision layer. The resulting portfolios differ materially from the archived holdings, yet BAB exposure explains a substantial share of their median FF5-plus-momentum alpha. One short-window result remains after multiplicity adjustment, so the replay qualifies rather than merely reinforces the proxy conclusion.

This pattern is consequential because the source papers generally do not ask the spanning question. Of the \SourceBenchmarkVerifiedCount mapped papers whose full text we verified, \SourceBenchmarkNoRegressionCount report no asset-pricing regression, one reports multifactor loadings without an intercept, \SourceBenchmarkAdjustedAlphaCount report factor-adjusted alpha, and \SourceBenchmarkJKPCount use JKP132. Consequently, return, Sharpe, predictive, or narrow-factor comparisons do not by themselves establish that a strategy is incremental to a broad anomaly library. The paper does not judge the scientific quality, methodological novelty, or practical value of all 98 screened works, and it does not infer failure for unavailable native agents. It asks the narrower question of whether the 50 disclosed common-task reconstructions retain alpha relative to JKP132.

One possible explanation for the observed overlap is that many systems prompt, retrieve, or search over financial concepts and symbolic factor expressions \cite{CensusACL2024findingsacl233,CensusArxiv250511122,CensusArxiv260515412}. Established anomaly ideas are common in financial text and factor libraries. Our design cannot determine whether any agent learned, retrieved, independently rediscovered, or arrived by another route at such content: the benchmark ladder and correlation map describe the reconstructed return paths, not the causal origin of an agent's strategy.

\textbf{Contributions.} We make five contributions. First, we provide a cutoff-bounded 98-work evidence waterfall that distinguishes 69 retained works, 40 reconstructed works, and 29 availability-only works without imputing failures as zero returns. Second, we evaluate all 50 retained reconstructions on one matched benchmark ladder, from CAPM through JKP132, rather than numerically backtesting only the five papers with the strongest source grounding. Third, we map every reconstructed strategy return series to all 132 JKP factors and summarize the factor families most closely aligned with those return paths. Fourth, we audit the benchmarks used in the mapped source papers and separate the primary factor-spanning result from a secondary 14-attempt reproducibility audit and other sensitivity analyses. Fifth, we replay a fully disclosed prompt-driven system and publish its holdings, return paths, costs, turnover, fitted values, residuals, penalties, and loadings.

\textbf{One-sentence takeaway.} For the 50 post-hoc reconstructions, alpha under narrow benchmarks largely attenuates under JKP132 and no familywise-positive residual remains; this concerns the reconstructions, not the novelty or value of the source papers or unavailable agents.

\subsection{Common U.S. task}

The monthly U.S. panel runs from August 2001 through December 2024. At formation month $t$, the universe $\mathcal U_t$ contains the 1,000 largest positive-market-equity common stocks, and mapping $i$ produces point-in-time score $s_{ijt}=g_i(x_{jt})$. For the standard value-weighted long--short mapping, with top and bottom deciles $H_{it}$ and $L_{it}$ and market equity $m_{jt}>0$, target weights are
\begin{equation}
w_{ijt}=\begin{cases}
m_{jt}/\sum_{k\in H_{it}}m_{kt}, & j\in H_{it},\\
-m_{jt}/\sum_{k\in L_{it}}m_{kt}, & j\in L_{it},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
Labeled long-only and sparse top-$k$ mappings preserve their stated rules. Formation never conditions on next-month return availability. Missing held returns receive zero in the primary analysis without renormalizing surviving weights; an adverse unit-move stress is reported separately.

Turnover restores target exposure from drifted pre-trade weights. With total security return $R^{\mathrm{tot}}_{j,t}$ and held excess return $R^{e,*}_{j,t+1}$,
\begin{align}
\widetilde w_{ij,t^-}&=\frac{w_{ij,t-1}(1+R^{\mathrm{tot}}_{j,t})}
 {1+\sum_k w_{ik,t-1}R^{\mathrm{tot}}_{k,t}},
&\tau_{it}&=\sum_j|w_{ijt}-\widetilde w_{ij,t^-}|,\notag\\
r^{\mathrm{net}}_{i,t+1}(c)&=\sum_j w_{ijt}R^{e,*}_{j,t+1}-c\tau_{it},
&&c\in\{0,5,10,25,50\}\times10^{-4}.
\label{eq:net-return}
\end{align}
The primary one-way rate is \bps{10}. This linear cost model omits borrow, financing, nonlinear impact, and capacity; gross benchmark factors make the comparison conservative.

\subsection{Public-prompt replay}

GuruAgents is evaluated a second way because its public repository identifies the decision model, temperature-zero prompts, deterministic finance-tool observations, and archived portfolios. We execute \PromptReplaySuccessfulCount/\PromptReplayExperimentCount agent--quarter--mode cells using \texttt{openai/gpt-4o} through OpenRouter at a total API cost of \PromptReplayCostUSD. The \emph{archived-final} mode supplies the completed source tool transcript before the final allocation decision; \emph{tool-routing} starts from the exact system prompt and user request and lets the model call the corresponding archived tools. Mean ticker-set Jaccard agreement with the authors' holdings is \PromptReplayArchivedJaccard and \PromptReplayToolJaccard, respectively, so the model decision is not interchangeable with either the archived portfolio or our characteristic proxy.

The replay produces \PromptReplayPathCount paths: both modes for five agents and an equal-weight ensemble in two source archives. Quarter-end formations execute at the first subsequent trading close, weights drift between quarterly rebalances, dividend-adjusted closes determine gross returns, and \bps{10} times one-way traded notional determines net returns. The matched attribution ladder restricts every model to the \PromptReplayMatchedPathCount long-archive paths and the same \PromptReplayFactorMonthCount realization months (April 2022--December 2024). It uses official U.S. CAPM, FF3, and FF5-plus-momentum returns, then adds the JKP BAB return alone and a fixed low-risk block containing BAB, conventional and Dimson beta, downside beta, idiosyncratic and realized volatility, and quality-safety returns. Unrestricted JKP132 OLS requires 134 parameters including the intercept and is not identified; we therefore report a pre-2022 factor-only PCA compression and an explicitly exploratory, noncausal leave-one-month-out ridge fit with all 133 returns.

\subsection{Matched benchmark ladder and factor overlap}

The headline analysis restricts attention to all 50 strategies associated with the 40 reconstructed retained works and fixes their net returns, 126 evaluation months (August 2011--January 2022), and inference across four benchmarks. For benchmark $b$, slopes use a rolling 120-month window. The broad model contains the market and 132 JKP characteristic returns; the six FF5-plus-momentum analogues remain unpenalized, and ridge penalizes the other 127 slopes. The penalty $\lambda\in\{0.1,1,10,100,1000\}$ minimizes mean-squared error over the last 24 months of each training window before refitting on all 120 months. CAPM, FF3, and FF5 plus momentum use unpenalized rolling OLS. For each test month,
\begin{align}
\widehat\theta_{it}^{(b)}(\lambda)
&=\arg\min_{\theta=(a,\beta)}
 \left\{\sum_{s=t-120}^{t-1}(r_{is}-a-\beta^\top f_s^{(b)})^2\right.\notag\\
&\hspace{7em}\left.+\lambda\|P_b\beta\|_2^2\right\},\\
e_{it}^{(b)}&=r_{it}-\widehat\beta_{it}^{(b)\top}f_t^{(b)},
&\widehat\alpha_i^{(b)}&=12\,\overline e_i^{(b)},
\label{eq:rolling-spanning}
\end{align}
where $P_b$ exempts the six base returns from penalization. Omitting the fitted intercept from $e_{it}^{(b)}$ makes its mean the factor-unexplained return. HAC statistics use $L=4$; Holm and a 5,000-replication moving-block maximum-$|t|$ procedure control the 50-strategy family conditional on the mappings.

To identify familiar economic content directly, we correlate each strategy with every JKP factor over the 246 common months:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{ik}=\operatorname{Corr}(r_{i},f^{\mathrm{JKP}}_{k}),
\qquad k_i^*=\arg\max_{1\le k\le132}|\rho_{ik}|.
\label{eq:factor-match}
\end{equation}
This nearest-factor statistic is descriptive. Correlation does not identify the agent's inputs or prove that a source paper copied a factor; it tests whether the reconstructed return path resembles a known factor return.

\subsection{What changes when the LLM is actually prompted?}

The prompt replay confirms that the language-model decision layer is empirically material. The two replay modes reproduce only 10 and 9 of 95 archived ticker orderings exactly, and their mean ticker-set agreement is well below one. The best short-window net path is the archived-final Buffett sleeve, with \PromptReplayBestReturnPct annualized geometric return and a \PromptReplayBestSharpe Sharpe ratio over 36 months. Across replay--author common samples, however, mean return correlation is 0.952; both portfolios use the same archived signals and compiled Nasdaq source universe, so this is not an independent out-of-sample validation.

Table~\ref{tab:guru-prompt-attribution} gives the direct factor test. Official FF5 plus momentum leaves median alpha of \PromptReplayFFFiveMomMedianAlphaPct. The signs are economically coherent. Risk-aware and stable-quality reconstructions often align with low-beta or low-volatility returns. Their correlations with positively oriented volatility factors can therefore be negative. Trend and breakout reconstructions align positively with 52-week-high and momentum factors, while fundamental-style reconstructions align with quality or valuation. These patterns show that JKP132 contains return series closely related to the economic motifs implemented in the common-task mappings; they do not identify what any native agent learned or generated.

The source benchmark audit shows that the mapped papers generally do not report a directly comparable spanning analysis. Among \SourceBenchmarkVerifiedCount verified mapped papers, \SourceBenchmarkNoRegressionCount contain no asset-pricing factor regression and \SourceBenchmarkLoadingsOnlyCount reports loadings without a factor-adjusted intercept; none reports factor-adjusted alpha or uses JKP132. Source evaluations instead emphasize predictive scores, cumulative or excess returns, Sharpe ratios, buy-and-hold comparisons, agent baselines, or factor loadings. Those outcomes can establish usefulness relative to a chosen task, but they do not by themselves establish incremental alpha beyond the factor zoo.

\subsection{Fidelity, costs, and secondary diagnostics}

The longer 2001--2024 six-factor exercise provides an implementation sensitivity, not the headline matched ladder. Among the 13 source-grounded component tests, median annualized six-factor alpha is 0.88\%; one is nominally positive and none survives Holm within that subset. Across the broader 62-row ledger, which adds 12 mappings from excluded diagnostic sources, median alpha at \bps{10} is \USMedianAlphaPct and one researcher-authored AlphaAgent-inspired quality proxy survives conditional Holm. Trading costs weaken rather than create the result. Median six-factor alpha falls from \USMedianGrossAlphaPct gross to \USMedianAlphaAtFivePct at \bps{5} and \USMedianAlphaPct at \bps{10}; positive counts across 0/5/10/25/50 bp are \USPositiveAtZero/\USPositiveAtFive/\USPositiveAtTen/\USPositiveAtTwentyFive/\USPositiveAtFifty. An adverse missing-return stress leaves zero nominal or Holm positives. A separate 133-factor diagnostic over the 62-row ledger yields one nominal result but zero Holm or maximum-$|t|$ results.

The international extension is forensic rather than performance evidence. Thirty-five paths cross nonpositive NAV; security-level reconstruction shows that every event is dominated by one extreme positive return held short. Without independent validation of those returns and corporate actions, we retain the event ledger but exclude international alpha from the paper's conclusion.

\section{Interpretation and Limits}

The evidence supports a bounded statement about the implemented mappings. LLM-agent methods may produce economically meaningful returns and may contribute methodological or practical value through factor discovery, formula search, or portfolio construction. In this study, however, the reconstructed family has negative median residual return against JKP132 and no multiplicity-adjusted positive result. The correlation evidence relates that attenuation to familiar factor families rather than treating a larger benchmark as an unexplained statistical black box.

Any explanation for the overlap remains a hypothesis. Financial LLM systems operate on pretrained knowledge, prompts, retrieved text, hand-specified operators, or factor DSLs that can foreground established anomalies. When evaluation uses buy-and-hold, Sharpe, predictive metrics, or FF3/FF5, relevant broad-factor exposure may remain unmeasured. Our audit documents the absence of comparable broad adjustment in the verified source papers and measures overlap in the reconstructed returns. It does not observe proprietary pretraining corpora, retrieval logs, or causal pathways, and it does not attribute any strategy to memorization, retrieval, or rediscovery.

Three limitations prevent a stronger agent-level claim. First, among the 50 headline mappings, 37 are favorable in-spirit reconstructions, 12 are source-grounded component tests, and only one adapts released code; none of those 50 is a native-agent return replication. The separate GuruAgents study replays its public prompting procedure, but uses archived deterministic tool observations and a compiled Nasdaq universe rather than independently rebuilding the source data. Second, mappings and the benchmark ladder were frozen after U.S. outcomes had been inspected and had no independent second coder. Holm and bootstrap procedures cover sampling multiplicity across realized formulas, not mapping discretion, prior factor mining, or publication selection \cite{HarveyLiuZhu2016}. Third, common-task portfolios omit language, memory, retrieval, debate, native timing, dynamic allocation, non-equities, and execution. A reconstruction failure cannot prove native-agent failure, and reconstruction success cannot be attributed to an agent.

\section{Reproducibility and Secondary Code Audit}

The secondary repository audit targets 14 implementations: eight retained systems and six excluded diagnostic works. It reproduces zero native agents. One released QuantEvolver seed expression supports a testable code-backed adaptation with annualized six-factor alpha of 0.33\% ($t=0.65$); the other 13 attempts have recorded output, input/adapter, task, licensing, or executability blockers and do not enter a performance denominator. The separately reported GuruAgents prompt replay is not counted as a native code execution or as one of these 14 outcomes. Keeping this audit secondary avoids conflating repository availability with the 40-paper/50-strategy spanning analysis.
This 14-target execution inventory is narrower than the paper-level route audit: among the \PublicCodeRouteWorkCount code-available retained works, \PublicCodeTargetedWorkCount have targeted records and \PublicCodeStaticBlockerWorkCount have precise static common-task blockers but were not execution targets. In both cases, any common-task proxy is secondary.

\section{Conclusion}

Financial LLM-agent papers often identify profitable strategies, and this study does not dispute returns measured under their stated tasks and benchmarks. Within the 50 retained common-task reconstructions from 40 papers, median annualized residual return falls from 9.97\% under CAPM to $-1.69\%$ under the market plus JKP132, and the Holm-adjusted discovery count falls to zero. The median reconstruction has 0.81 absolute correlation with its closest JKP factor, with the strongest concentration in low-risk, 52-week-high, volatility, quality, momentum, and valuation families. These findings do not rank the scientific quality, methodological novelty, or practical value of the 98 screened works.

The direct GuruAgents replay narrows the external-validity concern. Replaying the public GPT-4o procedure rather than substituting a formula produces materially different holdings, yet adding BAB alone cuts the matched median FF5-plus-momentum alpha by 3.20 percentage points and attenuates 11 of 12 paths. One Buffett replay remains Holm-positive through the identified low-risk and compressed-JKP specifications, while none survives the exploratory all-JKP ridge. The responsible conclusion is therefore not that prompting can never add alpha, nor that broad factors conclusively absorb this short replay; it is that faithful prompting still requires explicit factor attribution, adequate history, costs, and multiplicity control.

The evidence supports a methodological recommendation rather than a causal verdict about agents. Claims of incremental LLM-generated alpha should report broad factor spanning, factor overlap, multiplicity correction, costs, and dated reproducible outputs. Independent native-agent replications and outcome-blind mapping studies are still needed to determine how broadly the reconstruction-level result extends to the underlying systems.
