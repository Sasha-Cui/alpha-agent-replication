\begin{abstract}
Financial-agent papers increasingly report profitable factors and trading policies, yet released evidence rarely permits independent alpha testing. We ask the narrower question: what do public artifacts substantiate? A cutoff-bounded systematic screen yields 103 candidate lineages backed by 98 distinct cited works; frozen rules retain 69 formula-discovery or trading works and exclude 29 visible benchmarks, comparators, audits, or adjacent works. Forty retained works receive 50 good-faith common-task mappings; 29 remain availability-only. A separate 14-attempt code audit yields no native-agent replication and one testable released seed-expression adaptation, which does not beat a six-factor benchmark. Thirteen mappings from five works are source-grounded component tests: median annualized alpha is 0.88\%, one is nominally positive, and none survives Holm within that subset. The other 49 mappings in the 62-portfolio family are favorable narrative translations whose failures cannot be charged to their sources. At 10-basis-point one-way costs, family median alpha is \USMedianAlphaPct versus \USMedianGrossAlphaPct gross; one conditional Holm result is specification-fragile and no simultaneous lower bound exceeds two percentage points. A post-hoc 133-factor diagnostic yields no familywise result. Because mappings were frozen after U.S. outcomes were inspected and had no independent second coder, these statistics are descriptive conditional diagnostics, not confirmatory inference. The evidence establishes a broad artifact gap and limited support from reconstructable components; it does not establish that financial agents as a class lack alpha.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Financial AI systems now search factor expressions, debate investment theses, retrieve news, and revise portfolios. Their evaluations often report Sharpe ratios, raw trading gains, benchmark outperformance, or alpha \cite{CensusACL2024findingsacl233,CensusArxiv250216789,CensusACL2025emnlpdemos14,CensusArxiv260515412,CensusDOI10114537682923770377}. The scientific question is not whether these architectures are interesting. It is whether the evidence released with them supports the claimed incremental return.

That question contains two different failure modes. A system may be impossible to evaluate because its code, data adapter, trained state, or dated output is unavailable. Alternatively, a testable return path may exist but fail an asset-pricing benchmark. Treating both as zero alpha is wrong; stopping at ``not reproducible'' is also incomplete. We therefore use the highest-fidelity feasible route and label the estimand at every step. Native or code-backed returns, when available, test a released implementation. When they are unavailable, reconstruction must give the public idea a serious opportunity to work: preserve source-supported content, choose a favorable reasonable implementation, encode ambiguity as variants, and disclose every substitution. A narrative mapping can stress-test an economic story, but its failure cannot be charged to the source.

This distinction narrows the claim. We do not test whether financial agents as a class can discover alpha. We test whether their \emph{public evidence} establishes alpha under an independently specified common task. Our U.S. portfolio results are retrospective historical spanning diagnostics conditional on researcher-authored mappings. They do not demonstrate prospective discovery and cannot correct for prior factor mining or publication selection \cite{HarveyLiuZhu2016}.

We contribute a cutoff-bounded 98-work evidence waterfall; a direct-code audit separating one testable expression adaptation from non-evaluable attempts; a good-faith reconstruction contract with an explicit negative-inference boundary; and a 62-row U.S. ledger with cost, missing-return, spanning, and multiplicity diagnostics. The finding is not ``the papers fail.'' Released evidence generally does not independently establish the claimed alpha: 13 source-grounded components provide limited common-task evidence, while 49 narrative translations remain exploratory.

\subsection{Mechanism mapping and disclosed discretion}

When direct evaluation is unavailable, we map public descriptions to point-in-time JKP characteristics \cite{JensenKellyPedersen2023}. This formalizes a good-faith, in-spirit reconstruction: preserve the strongest supported content, disclose every substitution, and give the idea the benefit of the doubt rather than construct an easy-to-reject surrogate.

The eight-step contract records a claim card; prioritizes released expressions, equations, examples/rules, narratives, then summaries; preserves supported ingredients, signs, lags, interactions, and constraints; and resolves ambiguity favorably without return tuning. Source-stated direction, explicit long-only or sparse top-$k$ rules, and both gross and net results are preserved. Reasonable alternatives should be coded before outcomes and remain in the multiplicity family; no sign, candidate, or variant may be selected using test returns.

A source-protection rule lets released expressions, named rules, and examples speak only to that component under the common task. Narrative translations are favorable stress tests whose failure cannot count as evidence against the source. Preferred review uses a second, outcome-blind coder. The public card records supported and supplied elements, favorable choices, omissions, alternatives, freeze/outcome/reviewer status, and the negative-evidence boundary.

The mappings predate this contract, so we apply only its source-protection rule retrospectively. Each row records the source, rationale, score, portfolio rule, support, omissions, favorable choices, and permitted interpretation. Table~\ref{tab:mapping} labels narrative translations M0 even when favorable.

Combining M1 and M2 yields 13 source-grounded component tests. Their median six-factor alpha is 0.88\%; one is nominally positive and none survives Holm within the 13-test subset. Under the post-hoc broad benchmark, their median alpha is $-2.00\%$, with zero nominal or Holm-positive results. The 49 M0 rows have median alpha $-0.10\%$ and contain the full family's sole Holm result; both positive and negative M0 outcomes belong to our translations, not to the source systems.

The ledger makes three adverse facts explicit. First, \MappingNarrativeCount mappings are narrative translations and therefore barred from source-level negative inference; \MappingPartialCount have partial support and only one uses a released expression. Second, the registry was frozen after U.S. outcomes had been inspected, so its immutable hash does not make coding outcome-blind. Third, no independent second coder was used. The mapping choice set is unknown; Holm and bootstrap calculations therefore quantify sampling variation only conditional on these 62 realized formulas. We treat every resulting $p$-value and interval as descriptive, not confirmatory, even when a conditional adjustment is below 5\%.

We can measure only a lower bound on that discretion. Five sources already have multiple coded mappings, covering \MappingAlternativeCandidateCount candidates. Selecting one mapping per source yields \MappingCombinationCount combinations; the same AlphaAgent-inspired quality proxy is the sole Holm discovery in every combination. This does not validate singly mapped formulas, nor does it replace independent coding. It shows only that the headline count is unchanged within the alternatives that happened to be coded.

\subsection{Common U.S. task and descriptive estimators}

The primary panel is monthly U.S. common equity from August 2001 through December 2024. At each formation month we retain the 1,000 largest positive-market-equity securities, compute signals using information available at formation, and hold for the next month. Most mappings are value-weighted top-minus-bottom deciles; long-only mappings are labeled. Long and short legs each carry unit notional. The locked primary policy assigns zero at the frozen weight when a held next-month return is missing; this avoids selecting or renormalizing holdings using future coverage. We report missing gross-weight exposure and a deliberately severe stress assigning $-100\%$ to a missing long return and $+100\%$ to a missing short return. It is not an expected delisting-return estimate.

Turnover uses drifted pre-trade weights. The locked \bps{10} one-way charge is a transparent reference point, not a security-specific estimate; we therefore report the entire 0/5/10/25/50-bp curve and candidate break-even costs. Borrow, financing, locate constraints, nonlinear impact, and capacity are omitted. Benchmark factors are gross, so costed-candidate alpha is a deliberately asymmetric, conservative comparison rather than two equally costed tradable products.

For candidate $i$ we estimate
\[
r_{i,t}^{\mathrm{net}}=\alpha_i+\beta_i' f_t+\varepsilon_{i,t}.
\]
The primary Newey--West lag is the automatic rule $\lfloor4(T/100)^{2/9}\rfloor$ (four at $T=281$) \cite{NeweyWest1987}. We report two-sided nominal tests, Holm correction across all 62 candidates \cite{Holm1979}, and paired moving-block maximum-$|t|$ inference with 2,000 bootstrap replications and a six-month block. Block lengths three and twelve and HAC lags 0, 3, 6, and 12 are sensitivities. A simultaneous lower bound of at least two annual percentage points is a pragmatic reporting threshold selected by the project owner after U.S. outcomes were available; it is not a preregistered decision rule.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Descriptive U.S. historical spanning exercise}

All 62 paths are executable for \USRegressionMonthCount months. At \bps{10}, median annualized six-factor alpha is \USMedianAlphaPct (interquartile range \USAlphaIQRPct); \USPositiveAlphaCount estimates are positive, \USNominalCount have nominal $p<.05$, and one has conditional Holm and maximum-$|t|$ values below .05. These are not confirmatory discoveries because mapping discretion is outside the multiplicity family. No simultaneous lower bound exceeds two percentage points. The largest estimate, 6.93\%, is a \emph{researcher-authored AlphaAgent-inspired quality proxy}; the software did not generate it, and its simultaneous lower bound is 0.48\%.

The conditional result is fragile to serial-correlation choices. Across fixed Newey--West lags 0/3/6/12, nominal positive counts are \USHACLagNominalCounts and Holm counts are \USHACLagHolmCounts: the adjusted count is one only at lags 3 and 6. Block-length changes leave the maximum-statistic count at one, but cannot cure mapping or publication-selection uncertainty.

Costs change the economic sign but not the core evidentiary limitation. Median alpha moves from \USMedianGrossAlphaPct gross to \USMedianAlphaAtFivePct at \bps{5} and \USMedianAlphaPct at \bps{10}; positive counts across 0/5/10/25/50 bp are \USPositiveAtZero/\USPositiveAtFive/\USPositiveAtTen/\USPositiveAtTwentyFive/\USPositiveAtFifty, while conditional Holm counts are \USCostHolmCounts. Among 46 gross-positive mappings, median break-even cost is \USGrossPositiveBreakEvenMedianBps bp (IQR \USGrossPositiveBreakEvenIQRBps). Thus the median's sign depends on costs, but gross returns still yield only one conditional adjusted result.

Missing-return exposure is small in weight but economically nontrivial under an extreme imputation. The median mapping's mean missing gross-weight share is \USMedianCandidateMeanMissingPct (maximum across mappings \USMaxCandidateMeanMissingPct). The position-adverse unit-move stress changes family median alpha to \USAdverseMissingMedianAlphaPct, leaves \USAdverseMissingPositiveCount positive estimates, and leaves zero nominal or conditional Holm positives. This does not identify delisting returns; it shows that zero fill is consequential and that adverse missing outcomes do not create evidence of alpha.

These results support two deliberately separated findings. The 13 source-grounded mappings provide no familywise evidence that their documented components add U.S. alpha under the common task. The other 49 show what happens to generous characteristic translations, but cannot be used to reject the originating papers. Neither result shows what an unavailable native agent would earn, and neither establishes prospective discovery.

\subsection{Post-hoc broad-factor diagnostic}

The post-hoc diagnostic uses the market plus 132 JKP factors. The source panel ends in December 2021; after shifting factors to return months and a 120-month trailing window, evaluation spans August 2011--January 2022 (\BroadEvaluationMonthCount months), not 2024.

Each month selects the ridge penalty on 96 past fit and 24 validation months, refits on all 120, and emits a one-month-ahead residual. The 5,000-replication block bootstrap resamples already-estimated residuals; it does \emph{not} rerun rolling estimation or penalty selection. Conditional on this possibly optimistic realized path and designed after primary outcomes, it is descriptive, not robustness confirmation.

There are \BroadPositiveAlphaCount positive estimates and one nominal result, but zero conditional Holm or maximum-$|t|$ results and zero simultaneous lower bounds above two percentage points. The best raw $p$-value is \BroadBestRawP; its Holm value is \BroadBestHolmP and maximum-statistic value is \BroadBestMaxTP. This is directionally consistent with spanning, but remains a post-hoc diagnostic over a truncated period.

\subsection{International extension: forensic result, not alpha evidence}

The international extension is excluded from headline performance inference. Thirty-five paths cross nonpositive NAV, producing \InternationalFailureEventCount events (31 France, five Canada, two Germany, one Britain, one Italy). Security-level reconstruction matches every event; in all \InternationalExtremeShortCount, one extreme positive return held short causes at least half the loss. Two France month-cells contain \InternationalTwoCellCount events.

Return caps of +100\%/+200\%/+500\%/+1,000\% leave 0/1/1/7 events, but are not admissible corrections. Corporate actions, return construction, stale prices, or short-weight interactions may be responsible. Without independent security-level validation, this is a data/implementation alarm; the ledger retains every event, identifier, weight, return, contribution, error, and cap diagnostic.

\section{What the Evidence Does and Does Not Establish}

Scientific value lies in an auditable 1-of-14 code-backed performance path; a fairness rule giving supported components favorable tests while restricting attribution; and cost, missing-return, factor, and multiplicity diagnostics exposing the fragility of raw-return or nominal-test conclusions.

The design has firm limits. Many systems postdate the December 2024 return panel, and mappings were frozen in 2026 after outcome inspection. The exercise cannot distinguish discovery from repackaging of known characteristics. Holm adjustment covers sampling multiplicity only across the 62 realized formulas; it does not cover researcher mapping choices, the literature's search process, unpublished mappings, or publication selection. Without an independently coded, outcome-blind reconstruction, performance results remain descriptive.

Common-task comparability trades away language, memory, retrieval, debate, native timing/execution, dynamic allocation, non-equities, and tool adaptation. Mapping failure cannot prove native-agent failure; success cannot be attributed to an agent. The strongest-looking row is M0, precisely where attribution is forbidden.

Benchmark practice outside finance offers a model: ABC separates task/outcome validity and reporting \cite{ZhuEtAl2025ABC}; Terminal-Bench uses containerized coding tasks, executable tests, human solutions, and failure analysis \cite{MerrillEtAl2026TerminalBench}. Financial-agent releases additionally need pinned code/environments; dated signals, positions, or returns; universe, timing, cost, financing, model, prompt, and seed definitions; search breadth and failures; and provenance or ablation separating agent contribution from factor libraries and human search spaces.

\section{Conclusion}

Public artifacts do not establish broad, economically material U.S. financial-agent alpha. The 98-work screen narrows to 69 retained, 40 reconstructed, and five supporting component attribution. The code route yields zero native-agent replications, one testable seed, and no benchmark beater; 13 blockers are evidence failures, not negative returns. Of 13 grounded components, one is nominal and none conditionally Holm-positive; 49 narrative tests cannot count against their sources. The sole family conditional result is specification-fragile, no effect clears the materiality threshold, the adverse missing-return stress has no positive result, and the post-hoc broad diagnostic has no adjusted result. International performance is excluded after a plausibility failure.

The paper's contribution is therefore constructive rather than categorical. It supplies an evidence hierarchy and an inspectable good-faith workflow for giving unreleased ideas their strongest reasonable test, while stopping interpretation exactly where fidelity and design no longer permit attribution.
