\begin{abstract}
Most reported alpha claims for LLM financial agents do not hold up to independent scrutiny. A cutoff-bounded systematic screen yields 103 candidate lineages backed by 98 distinct cited works; frozen rules retain 69 formula-discovery or trading works and exclude 29 visible benchmarks, comparators, audits, or adjacent works. Forty retained works receive 50 good-faith common-task mappings; 29 remain availability-only. A separate 14-attempt code audit yields no native-agent replication and one testable released seed-expression adaptation, which does not beat a six-factor benchmark. Among 13 source-grounded component tests from five works, median annualized alpha is 0.88\%, one estimate is nominally positive, and none survives within-subset Holm correction. The other 49 mappings are favorable narrative translations. At 10-basis-point one-way costs, family median alpha is \USMedianAlphaPct versus \USMedianGrossAlphaPct gross; the sole conditional Holm result comes from a researcher-authored proxy, is specification-fragile, and has a simultaneous lower bound below two percentage points. A post-hoc 133-factor diagnostic yields no familywise result. The literature therefore substantially overstates the evidence for LLM-generated alpha: native agents are rarely reproducible, and the testable evidence does not demonstrate robust, economically material alpha. Because mappings were frozen after U.S. outcomes were inspected and had no independent second coder, these statistics are descriptive conditional diagnostics, not confirmatory inference. This conclusion concerns the reported evidence, not whether every unavailable agent must fail.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM-agent papers increasingly claim that their systems can discover profitable factors, outperform benchmarks, or generate investment alpha \cite{CensusACL2024findingsacl233,CensusArxiv250216789,CensusACL2025emnlpdemos14,CensusArxiv260515412,CensusDOI10114537682923770377}. Our audit finds that these claims generally do not withstand independent scrutiny. None of 14 targeted code audits reproduces a native agent, the closest source-grounded tests yield no familywise-adjusted evidence of alpha, and the few positive findings depend on researcher-authored proxies or fragile specifications.

Reported alpha can fail scrutiny in two ways. First, the system may be unevaluable because its code, data adapter, trained state, or dated output is unavailable. In that case, the alpha claim lacks reproducible support; we do not impute a zero return. Second, a testable return path may exist but fail an asset-pricing benchmark. We therefore use the highest-fidelity feasible route and label the estimand at every step. Native or code-backed returns test released implementations. When they are unavailable, reconstruction preserves source-supported content, chooses a favorable reasonable implementation, encodes ambiguity as variants, and discloses every substitution.

The target of inference is the reported evidence, not the unknowable performance of an unavailable agent. We ask whether public artifacts establish alpha under an independently specified common task. Our U.S. portfolios are retrospective spanning diagnostics conditional on researcher-authored mappings; they neither demonstrate prospective discovery nor correct for prior factor mining or publication selection \cite{HarveyLiuZhu2016}. This boundary does not weaken the conclusion: the literature presents alpha as an empirical result, and the released evidence generally fails to substantiate that result.

We make four contributions: a cutoff-bounded 98-work evidence waterfall; a direct-code audit separating one testable expression adaptation from non-evaluable attempts; a good-faith reconstruction contract with an explicit attribution boundary; and a 62-row U.S. ledger with cost, missing-return, spanning, and multiplicity diagnostics. The central finding is simple: most reported alpha claims do not hold up. The agents are rarely reproducible, the closest source-grounded evidence has no adjusted alpha, and the broader diagnostic's lone adjusted result is a fragile researcher-authored proxy rather than agent output.

\subsection{Mechanism mapping and disclosed discretion}

When direct evaluation is unavailable, we map public descriptions to point-in-time JKP characteristics \cite{JensenKellyPedersen2023}. This formalizes a good-faith, in-spirit reconstruction: preserve the strongest supported content, disclose every substitution, and give the idea the benefit of the doubt rather than construct an easy-to-reject surrogate.

The eight-step contract records a claim card; prioritizes released expressions, equations, examples/rules, narratives, then summaries; preserves supported ingredients, signs, lags, interactions, and constraints; and resolves ambiguity favorably without return tuning. Source-stated direction, explicit long-only or sparse top-$k$ rules, and both gross and net results are preserved. Reasonable alternatives should be coded before outcomes and remain in the multiplicity family; no sign, candidate, or variant may be selected using test returns.

A source-protection rule lets released expressions, named rules, and examples speak only to that component under the common task. Narrative translations are favorable stress tests whose failure cannot count as evidence against the source. Preferred review uses a second, outcome-blind coder. The public card records supported and supplied elements, favorable choices, omissions, alternatives, freeze/outcome/reviewer status, and the negative-evidence boundary.

The mappings predate this contract, so we apply only its source-protection rule retrospectively. Each row records the source, rationale, score, portfolio rule, support, omissions, favorable choices, and permitted interpretation. Table~\ref{tab:mapping} labels narrative translations M0 even when favorable.

The second reason the claims do not hold up is that the closest testable content shows no robust alpha. Combining M1 and M2 yields 13 source-grounded component tests. Their median six-factor alpha is 0.88\%; one is nominally positive and none survives Holm within the 13-test subset. Under the post-hoc broad benchmark, median alpha is $-2.00\%$, with zero nominal or Holm-positive results. The 49 M0 rows do not rescue the claim: their median alpha is $-0.10\%$, and the sole full-family Holm result is a researcher-authored translation rather than agent output. Component tests do not reproduce native agents, but they show that the strongest reconstructable content does not deliver robust alpha under the common task.

The ledger makes three adverse facts explicit. First, \MappingNarrativeCount mappings are narrative translations and therefore barred from source-level negative inference; \MappingPartialCount have partial support and only one uses a released expression. Second, the registry was frozen after U.S. outcomes had been inspected, so its immutable hash does not make coding outcome-blind. Third, no independent second coder was used. The mapping choice set is unknown; Holm and bootstrap calculations therefore quantify sampling variation only conditional on these 62 realized formulas. We treat every resulting $p$-value and interval as descriptive, not confirmatory, even when a conditional adjustment is below 5\%.

We can measure only a lower bound on that discretion. Five sources already have multiple coded mappings, covering \MappingAlternativeCandidateCount candidates. Selecting one mapping per source yields \MappingCombinationCount combinations; the same AlphaAgent-inspired quality proxy is the sole Holm discovery in every combination. This does not validate singly mapped formulas, nor does it replace independent coding. It shows only that the headline count is unchanged within the alternatives that happened to be coded.

\subsection{Common U.S. task and descriptive estimators}

The primary panel is monthly U.S. common equity from August 2001 through December 2024. At formation month $t$, the universe $\mathcal U_t$ contains the 1,000 largest positive-market-equity securities, and mapping $i$ produces a point-in-time score $s_{ijt}=g_i(x_{jt})$. For the usual value-weighted long--short mapping, let $H_{it}$ and $L_{it}$ denote its top and bottom deciles and let $m_{jt}>0$ be market equity. The frozen target weights are
\begin{equation}
w_{ijt}=\begin{cases}
m_{jt}/\sum_{k\in H_{it}}m_{kt}, & j\in H_{it},\\
-m_{jt}/\sum_{k\in L_{it}}m_{kt}, & j\in L_{it},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
Thus each leg carries unit notional; labeled long-only and sparse top-$k$ mappings use their stated rules. Formation never conditions on next-month return availability. Define $R^{e,*}_{j,t+1}=R^e_{j,t+1}$ when the held excess return is observed and $R^{e,*}_{j,t+1}=0$ otherwise; weights are never renormalized using future coverage. A severe stress instead assigns $-\operatorname{sgn}(w_{ijt})$ to a missing return, or $-100\%$ for a long and $+100\%$ for a short; it is not a delisting-return estimate.

Turnover restores target exposure from drifted pre-trade weights. With total security return $R^{\mathrm{tot}}_{j,t}$,
\begin{align}
\widetilde w_{ij,t^-}
 &=\frac{w_{ij,t-1}(1+R^{\mathrm{tot}}_{j,t})}
 {1+\sum_k w_{ik,t-1}R^{\mathrm{tot}}_{k,t}}, \notag\\
\tau_{it}&=\sum_j\lvert w_{ijt}-\widetilde w_{ij,t^-}\rvert, \notag\\
r^{\mathrm{net}}_{i,t+1}(c)
 &=\sum_j w_{ijt}R^{e,*}_{j,t+1}-c\tau_{it}.
\label{eq:net-return}
\end{align}
The one-way rate $c$ spans $\{0,5,10,25,50\}\times10^{-4}$, with \bps{10} primary. This linear model omits borrow, financing, locate constraints, nonlinear impact, and capacity. Benchmark factors remain gross, so costed-candidate alpha is a deliberately conservative asymmetric comparison.

For each executable candidate, we estimate the six-factor model with $f_t\in\mathbb{R}^6$ and its HAC statistic
\begin{align}
r^{\mathrm{net}}_{i,t}&=\alpha_i+\beta_i^{\top}f_t+\varepsilon_{i,t}, \notag\\
T_i&=\frac{\widehat\alpha_i}
 {\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{NW}}(\widehat\alpha_i)},\qquad
L_T=\left\lfloor4(T/100)^{2/9}\right\rfloor .
\label{eq:factor-model}
\end{align}
The Newey--West rule gives $L_T=4$ at $T=281$ \cite{NeweyWest1987}. For ordered two-sided $p$-values $p_{(1)}\le\cdots\le p_{(m)}$, Holm adjustment is
\begin{equation}
p^{\mathrm H}_{(k)}=
\max_{\ell\le k}\min\{1,(m-\ell+1)p_{(\ell)}\},
\qquad m=62,
\label{eq:holm}
\end{equation}
where non-estimable planned paths enter with $p=1$ \cite{Holm1979}. Paired moving-block draws jointly resample candidate and factor months. For $B=2{,}000$, block length six, and fixed original HAC standard errors, the single-step family statistic is
\begin{align}
T_i^{*(b)}&=\frac{\widehat\alpha_i^{*(b)}-\widehat\alpha_i}
 {\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{NW}}(\widehat\alpha_i)},
&M^{*(b)}&=\max_i\lvert T_i^{*(b)}\rvert, \notag\\
\widehat p_i^{\max}
 &=\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf{1}\{M^{*(b)}\ge\lvert T_i\rvert\}}
 {B+1},
&\mathrm{LB}^{\mathrm{sim}}_i
 &=12\left(\widehat\alpha_i-q_{.95}\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{NW},i}\right).
\label{eq:max-t}
\end{align}
Here $q_{.95}$ is the 95th percentile of $M^{*}$. Block lengths three and twelve and HAC lags 0, 3, 6, and 12 are sensitivities. A simultaneous lower bound of at least two annual percentage points is a pragmatic reporting threshold selected after U.S. outcomes were available, not a preregistered rule.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Reported alpha under the common U.S. task}

The common-task results do not support the reported alpha. Among the 13 source-grounded component tests, median annualized six-factor alpha is 0.88\%; one estimate is nominally positive and none survives within-subset Holm correction. Expanding to all 62 executable paths does not change the conclusion. At \bps{10}, median alpha is \USMedianAlphaPct (interquartile range \USAlphaIQRPct); \USPositiveAlphaCount estimates are positive, \USNominalCount have nominal $p<.05$, and one has conditional Holm and maximum-$|t|$ values below .05. No simultaneous lower bound exceeds two percentage points. The sole adjusted result is the largest estimate, 6.93\%, from a \emph{researcher-authored AlphaAgent-inspired quality proxy}; the software did not generate it, and its simultaneous lower bound is only 0.48\%.

That lone result is statistically fragile. Across fixed Newey--West lags 0/3/6/12, nominal positive counts are \USHACLagNominalCounts and Holm counts are \USHACLagHolmCounts: the adjusted count is one only at lags 3 and 6. Block-length changes leave the maximum-statistic count at one, but they do not address mapping discretion or publication selection. A result that depends on a researcher-authored proxy and selected serial-correlation specifications is not robust evidence of agent-generated alpha.

Trading costs further weaken the claim. Median alpha falls from \USMedianGrossAlphaPct gross to \USMedianAlphaAtFivePct at \bps{5} and \USMedianAlphaPct at \bps{10}; positive counts across 0/5/10/25/50 bp are \USPositiveAtZero/\USPositiveAtFive/\USPositiveAtTen/\USPositiveAtTwentyFive/\USPositiveAtFifty, while conditional Holm counts are \USCostHolmCounts. Among 46 gross-positive mappings, median break-even cost is \USGrossPositiveBreakEvenMedianBps bp (IQR \USGrossPositiveBreakEvenIQRBps). Even gross returns produce only one conditional adjusted result, and no simultaneous lower bound reaches the two-percentage-point materiality threshold.

Missing-return exposure is small in weight but economically nontrivial under an extreme imputation. The median mapping's mean missing gross-weight share is \USMedianCandidateMeanMissingPct (maximum across mappings \USMaxCandidateMeanMissingPct). The position-adverse unit-move stress changes family median alpha to \USAdverseMissingMedianAlphaPct, leaves \USAdverseMissingPositiveCount positive estimates, and leaves zero nominal or conditional Holm positives. This does not identify delisting returns; it shows that zero fill is consequential and that adverse missing outcomes do not create evidence of alpha.

The closest source-grounded tests provide no familywise evidence of incremental return, and the broader family produces only one fragile result that cannot be attributed to an agent. These findings reject the strength of the published evidence; they do not assign zero returns to unavailable native systems.

\subsection{Post-hoc broad-factor diagnostic}

The broader factor benchmark does not rescue the alpha claim. It uses the market plus 132 JKP factors. The source panel ends in December 2021; after shifting factors to return months and a 120-month trailing window, evaluation spans August 2011--January 2022 (\BroadEvaluationMonthCount months), not 2024.

Each month selects the ridge penalty on 96 past fit and 24 validation months, refits on all 120, and emits a one-month-ahead residual. The 5,000-replication block bootstrap resamples already-estimated residuals; it does \emph{not} rerun rolling estimation or penalty selection. Conditional on this possibly optimistic realized path and designed after primary outcomes, it is descriptive, not robustness confirmation.

There are \BroadPositiveAlphaCount positive estimates and one nominal result, but zero conditional Holm or maximum-$|t|$ results and zero simultaneous lower bounds above two percentage points. The best raw $p$-value is \BroadBestRawP; its Holm value is \BroadBestHolmP and maximum-statistic value is \BroadBestMaxTP. This is directionally consistent with spanning, but remains a post-hoc diagnostic over a truncated period.

\subsection{International extension: forensic result, not alpha evidence}

The international extension is excluded from headline performance inference. Thirty-five paths cross nonpositive NAV, producing \InternationalFailureEventCount events (31 France, five Canada, two Germany, one Britain, one Italy). Security-level reconstruction matches every event; in all \InternationalExtremeShortCount, one extreme positive return held short causes at least half the loss. Two France month-cells contain \InternationalTwoCellCount events.

Return caps of +100\%/+200\%/+500\%/+1,000\% leave 0/1/1/7 events, but are not admissible corrections. Corporate actions, return construction, stale prices, or short-weight interactions may be responsible. Without independent security-level validation, this is a data/implementation alarm; the ledger retains every event, identifier, weight, return, contribution, error, and cap diagnostic.

\section{Why the Reported Alpha Claims Do Not Hold Up}

Three findings drive the conclusion. First, public releases do not reproduce any native agent in 14 targeted audits. Second, the 13 closest source-grounded component tests produce no familywise-adjusted alpha. Third, the sole adjusted result in the broader family is a researcher-authored proxy, disappears under some HAC choices, and never clears the familywise materiality threshold. The literature therefore substantially overstates the evidence for LLM-generated alpha.

This conclusion is strong but specific: most reported alpha claims do not survive independent evidentiary scrutiny. It is not a claim that every unavailable native agent must lose money. Many systems postdate the December 2024 return panel, and mappings were frozen in 2026 after outcome inspection. The exercise cannot distinguish discovery from repackaging of known characteristics. Holm adjustment covers sampling multiplicity only across the 62 realized formulas; it does not cover researcher mapping choices, the literature's search process, unpublished mappings, or publication selection. Without an independently coded, outcome-blind reconstruction, performance results remain descriptive.

Common-task comparability omits language, memory, retrieval, debate, native timing/execution, dynamic allocation, non-equities, and tool adaptation. A mapping failure cannot prove native-agent failure, and a mapping success cannot be attributed to an agent. This limitation matters for agent-level claims but does not rehabilitate the published evidence: authors who report alpha bear the burden of releasing artifacts that make the result independently testable.

Benchmark practice outside finance offers a model: ABC separates task/outcome validity and reporting \cite{ZhuEtAl2025ABC}; Terminal-Bench uses containerized coding tasks, executable tests, human solutions, and failure analysis \cite{MerrillEtAl2026TerminalBench}. Financial-agent releases additionally need pinned code/environments; dated signals, positions, or returns; universe, timing, cost, financing, model, prompt, and seed definitions; search breadth and failures; and provenance or ablation separating agent contribution from factor libraries and human search spaces.

\section{Conclusion}

Most reported alpha claims for LLM financial agents do not hold up to independent scrutiny. The 98-work screen narrows to 69 retained works, 40 reconstructed works, and five works supporting source-grounded component tests. The code route reproduces no native agent; its sole testable seed adaptation does not beat the benchmark. Among 13 source-grounded components, one estimate is nominally positive and none is Holm-positive. The broader 62-row family yields one conditional result, but it comes from a researcher-authored proxy, is HAC-fragile, and does not clear the materiality threshold. The post-hoc 133-factor diagnostic yields no adjusted result, while international performance is excluded after a plausibility failure.

The message is not that every LLM agent is incapable of producing alpha. It is that the literature's empirical case is much weaker than its claims: native systems are rarely reproducible, and the strongest testable evidence does not demonstrate robust, economically material alpha. The paper supplies an evidence hierarchy and an inspectable workflow for separating credible alpha evidence from unsupported performance claims.
