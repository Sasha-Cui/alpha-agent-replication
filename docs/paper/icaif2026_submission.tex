\begin{abstract}
Financial AI systems increasingly report profitable factors and trading policies, but the central scientific question is whether the claimed alpha survives independent reconstruction and standard asset-pricing controls. We answer it with a two-route audit. For claims with public code, we attempt to obtain dated U.S. equity returns directly; when the claimed system cannot produce an admissible return path, we implement the closest defensible version of its stated economic idea and label the resulting portfolio as a mechanism proxy, not an agent replication. The frozen census contains 67 formula-discovery or trading methods. Only 20 link a public artifact, three preserve dated native output, and just one of 14 targeted codebases yields a computable JKP-based seed-proxy path; it does not beat a market, value, size, investment, profitability, and momentum benchmark. We translate 51 of 55 claim-bearing sources into 62 frozen U.S. portfolios. Net of 10-basis-point one-way costs, median annualized six-factor alpha is $-0.05\%$; six estimates are nominally positive, one survives familywise inference, and none has a simultaneous lower bound above two percentage points. A post-hoc rolling control using the market and all 132 JKP characteristic factors leaves one nominal result and no familywise discovery. The evidence does not support a broad claim that public financial agents discover new, economically material U.S. equity alpha. It instead identifies a narrow set of familiar quality, profitability, value, momentum, and low-risk composites whose apparent performance depends on benchmark breadth, costs, multiplicity, and geography.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models and multi-agent systems now generate factor expressions, debate investment theses, retrieve news, call analytical tools, and revise portfolios. Many papers evaluate these systems by reporting alpha, Sharpe ratios, or trading gains \cite{TangEtAl2025AlphaAgent,LiEtAl2024FAMA,WangEtAl2025AlphaGPT,ZhangEtAl2026QuantEvolver,DuanEtAl2025FactorMAD}. The economically important question is simpler than the surrounding architectures: \emph{do the reported systems contain alpha that survives independent scrutiny?}

That question cannot be answered by a repository census alone. If code and dated outputs are available, the strongest test is to rerun the claimed strategy. If they are not, ``not reproducible'' is an intermediate finding, not the end of the analysis. The next best test is to reconstruct the published rule or economic mechanism as closely as public evidence permits, then ask whether that reconstruction earns net abnormal returns under a common evaluator. The fidelity loss must be explicit: a deterministic value--quality portfolio is not an LLM agent, but it can still test whether the economic content offered to explain the agent's return is incremental to known factors.

We operationalize this logic in two routes (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The \emph{direct-code route} attempts to produce dated U.S. equity returns from public implementations and approved local inputs. The \emph{reconstruction route} applies when native execution is unavailable or inadmissible: paper text, repository documentation, and released formulas are translated into frozen, point-in-time characteristic portfolios. Both routes terminate in an alpha test; missing code determines which route is possible, not whether the claim is examined.

Three research questions follow. \textbf{RQ1}: How often can public financial-agent claims be converted directly into dated return evidence? \textbf{RQ2}: When native evidence is absent, do closest-defensible reconstructions earn net U.S. equity alpha after costs, factor controls, and search-wide inference? \textbf{RQ3}: Which apparent survivors remain after a broad JKP factor control, and how do the same mechanisms behave outside the United States?

The answers are substantially negative but scientifically useful. Among 67 formula-discovery or trading methods, 20 link an artifact, 18 remain reachable, and three preserve dated output. Of 14 targeted public-code implementations, only a QuantEvolver seed-proxy path produces admissible JKP-based numerical returns; its annualized six-factor alpha is 0.33\% ($t=0.651$), and no direct-code row passes the benchmark gate. We then translate 51 of 55 claim-bearing sources into 62 U.S. mechanism proxies. All execute, yet at \bps{10} one-way costs the median six-factor alpha is \USMedianAlphaPct{}, only the AlphaAgent-inspired quality proxy survives Holm and paired maximum-$|t|$ inference, and its simultaneous lower bound is only \USBestSimultaneousLowerPct{}. A rolling post-hoc control that nests the six primary factors and regularizes the remaining 127 JKP factors yields no familywise discovery. Thus the evidence does not support a general public-agent alpha claim, even after we replace missing native systems with favorable, economically recognizable reconstructions.

The contribution is not a leaderboard. It is an empirical adjudication design for heterogeneous claims: pursue native evidence where possible, continue with the closest transparent reconstruction where necessary, retain all attempts in the denominator, and separate agent novelty from the return of familiar factor composites. This connects the reproducibility question to the economic one rather than allowing either to substitute for the other.

\section{Related Work and Evidentiary Standard}

Financial-agent research spans formula mining \cite{TangEtAl2025AlphaAgent,LiEtAl2024FAMA,WangEtAl2025AlphaGPT,WangEtAl2026FactorMiner,ZhangEtAl2026QuantEvolver}, multi-agent factor debate \cite{DuanEtAl2025FactorMAD}, and sequential portfolio systems \cite{XiaoEtAl2024TradingAgents,YuEtAl2024FinMem,KimEtAl2025GuruAgents}. Dedicated benchmarks and surveys report leakage, unstable reasoning, concentrated styles, and weak long-horizon performance in particular settings \cite{alphabench2026,DingEtAl2026TradingSurvey,ChenEtAl2025StockBench,LiEtAl2026FINSABER,LuoEtAl2026AlphaQT,ZhuEtAl2026KTDFin,ZhaoChenSu2026PortBench}. Our question differs: rather than compare language-model scores, we ask whether the return claim associated with each system survives a common asset-pricing test.

We distinguish four evidence levels. A \emph{native replication} reruns the public implementation, inputs, portfolio rule, and claimed target without an economically material substitution. A \emph{code-backed adapted test} retains released expressions or seed logic but supplies the common data adapter and evaluator. An \emph{independent reimplementation} ports a sufficiently explicit rule. A \emph{mechanism proxy} maps a documented idea to available characteristics while omitting central language, search, memory, or execution components. Only the first level can reproduce a native agent claim; the lower levels answer the narrower question of whether its released economic content contains alpha. Common-task methodology supports comparability only when this estimand boundary remains visible \cite{HellumEtAl2025CommonTask}.

Large strategy families create a second evidentiary requirement. Nominal $t$-statistics are not discoveries when dozens of correlated formulas were examined \cite{HarveyLiuZhu2016,BaileyEtAl2017PBO}. We therefore retain the entire frozen family, deduct costs, report effect sizes, and use Holm and paired maximum-statistic inference. A result is economically confirmed only when its simultaneous lower confidence bound exceeds two annualized percentage points.

\section{Research Design}

\subsection{Frozen census and direct-code attempts}

The public cutoff is 2 August 2026. Searches cover arXiv categories in AI, language, learning, computational finance, portfolio management, statistics, and trading; ACL Anthology, OpenReview, ACM Digital Library, and SSRN; backward and forward citation chains from prominent benchmarks; and exact-title or author-linked GitHub artifacts. Crossref and OpenAlex support deduplication. The unit is a named system lineage rather than a paper or repository.

The census contains \SystemCount{} lineages: 29 formula-discovery systems, 38 sequential trading or portfolio systems, 23 benchmarks or audits, five community systems, and eight mechanistic comparators. The first two strata form the fixed 67-method denominator. Table~\ref{tab:evidence} reports nested public-evidence states; an unavailable artifact is never assigned a zero return.

The targeted execution audit contains 14 public implementations tied to the claim corpus: 12 real public-code repositories, one ambiguous unofficial repository, and one placeholder. We inspect the released runner, data interface, strategy output, and claimed evaluation surface, then attempt the closest U.S.-JKP execution permitted by public materials and approved inputs. Outcomes distinguish missing return adapters, incompatible tasks, unavailable historical services, invalid outputs, and valid but unsuccessful tests. We do not contact authors, infer licenses, use paper-shipped returns from unapproved universes, buy data, or fabricate missing signals.

\subsection{Closest-defensible reconstruction}

When direct execution cannot produce admissible returns, we read the paper and repository evidence and translate the stated idea into the closest available U.S.-equity characteristic rule. Fifty-one of 55 sources are mappable; four concern a benchmark without a strategy, crypto, general tooling, or no alpha rule. The resulting 62 candidates include 56 value-weighted long--short decile portfolios, four long-only top-decile portfolios, one equal-weighted top-five portfolio, and one trailing-Sharpe sleeve selector.

Every ingredient, sign, strategy direction, portfolio rule, source label, and family member is frozen. Examples include the exact value--profitability--momentum motif associated with FAMA, the Greenblatt and Graham screens associated with GuruAgents, and a quality--cash-flow--safety formulation associated with AlphaAgent. These are deliberately favorable economic translations: they preserve the claimed intuition while removing stochastic language generation, memory, debate, and tool use. Consequently, success can establish that a documented mechanism has incremental returns; it cannot establish that an LLM discovered the mechanism or caused the return.

\subsection{Primary U.S. universe, portfolios, and costs}

The main universe is U.S. equities. We use point-in-time security characteristics from the Jensen--Kelly--Pedersen (JKP) data build \cite{JensenKellyPedersen2023,JKPGlobalFactorData2026}. Its U.S. security history is built from CRSP through WRDS. Signals observed at month-end are paired only with the supplied next consecutive month's excess return, \texttt{ret\_exc\_lead1m}. Eligible securities require a valid formation date, positive market equity, and all characteristics used by the score; future-return availability is never a formation filter. Within each month we retain the largest 1,000 eligible securities.

Long--short strategies buy the highest score decile and short the lowest, value weighted, with at least 20 securities per side. Long-only rules follow their frozen top-decile or top-five specification. Turnover compares target weights with drifted pre-trade weights using one post-return strategy-NAV denominator. Candidate returns are net of \bps{10} one-way costs; 0, 5, 25, and \bps{50} are sensitivities. A strategy path at or below a $-100\%$ monthly total return is stopped rather than clipped or recapitalized.

\subsection{Primary and broad factor controls}

For proxy $j$ we first estimate
\begin{equation}
  r^n_{j,t}=\alpha_j+\beta_j^{\top}f_t+u_{j,t},
  \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
where $f_t$ contains a country-local market return and value, size, investment, profitability, and momentum factor returns constructed under the same portfolio rules. The U.S. common sample contains \USRegressionMonthCount{} months from \USRegressionStartMonth{} through \USRegressionEndMonth{}. The endpoint is $12\widehat{\alpha}_j$.

Because 132 JKP characteristic factors are central to the novelty question, we add a post-hoc broad-span diagnostic that avoids the legacy underdetermined regression. The earlier same-sample fit used 134 regressors on 270 months and therefore cannot support a confirmatory claim. In the replacement diagnostic, the market and five primary factors are always unpenalized; the remaining 127 JKP factor loadings use ridge regularization. At each evaluation month, loadings use only the previous 120 months. The penalty is selected separately for each candidate from $\{0.1,1,10,100,1000\}$ using the first 96 training months and the final 24 as a validation block, then refitted on all 120 months. The training intercept is not removed from the next-month residual, so the mean residual remains an out-of-sample alpha estimand. This produces \BroadEvaluationMonthCount{} evaluation months from \BroadEvaluationStartMonth{} through \BroadEvaluationEndMonth{}.

The cached broad factor panel labels next-month returns by formation month, whereas the corrected evaluator labels them by realization month. We shift the panel forward once and require its market return to correlate at least 0.99 with the evaluator market; the observed correlation is \BroadMarketAlignmentCorrelation{}. This alignment rule and both rejected diagnostic runs are preserved in the audit trail. The broad result is explicitly exploratory because it was designed after primary U.S. outcomes were known.

\subsection{Inference and international extension}

Individual estimates use two-sided Newey--West inference \cite{NeweyWest1987}. Holm controls familywise error across all 62 candidates \cite{Holm1979}; BH and BY are descriptive \cite{BenjaminiHochberg1995,BenjaminiYekutieli2001}. A paired circular block bootstrap resamples months jointly across candidates and factors. Maximum-$|t|$ statistics give adjusted $p$-values and simultaneous intervals. Failed paths receive $p=1$ in Holm/FDR families. We require a positive point estimate for a positive claim and a simultaneous lower bound of at least two percentage points for economic confirmation.

Only after the U.S. analysis do we extend the frozen formulas to Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, and the United Kingdom. These files come from the same February 2026 processed JKP build; its non-U.S. security histories use Compustat Global through WRDS, and prices, market equity, return indices, total returns, and excess returns are converted to U.S. dollars by the upstream pipeline. Country sleeves are formed independently and equally weighted. This extension is secondary: an initial run revealed formation-timing, NAV-drift, country-pooling, and calendar defects, and a later run exposed nonpositive NAV. Corrected results apply the same limited-liability rule and preserve all superseded outputs. The extension is therefore a disclosed stress test, not a pristine holdout.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Direct reproduction yields no benchmark-beating path}

Public packaging is sparse: only 20 of 67 methods link an artifact, and three preserve dated output. More importantly, the 14-codebase execution audit produces just one admissible numerical U.S.-JKP result. QuantEvolver's released seed logic supports a code-backed return/Sharpe proxy, not a native trained-agent return. That path has annualized alpha 0.33\%, HAC $t=0.651$, information ratio 0.149, GRS $p=0.478$, and factor-span Sharpe lift 0.010. It does not pass the six-factor benchmark gate. The other 13 rows lack an admissible strategy return for reasons recorded separately from performance: missing return interfaces, incompatible frequency or market, unavailable services or data adapters, aggregate metrics without positions, or placeholder code.

This is where the reconstruction route matters. The direct result alone would justify ``public alpha not established,'' but not ``no economic content.'' Testing the 62 closest-defensible portfolios asks the stronger question: even if we give each public claim a transparent, favorable implementation of its documented mechanism, how much U.S. alpha remains?

\subsection{Primary U.S. result: one statistical survivor, none material}

Table~\ref{tab:mainresults} summarizes the answer. All 62 U.S. proxy paths execute. At \bps{10}, median annualized six-factor alpha is \USMedianAlphaPct{} (IQR \USAlphaIQRPct{}); \USPositiveAlphaCount{} estimates are positive and six are nominally positive. Only one survives both Holm ($p=.035$) and paired max-$|t|$ adjustment ($p=.024$). No simultaneous lower bound exceeds two percentage points.

The sole six-factor familywise survivor is linked to AlphaAgent. We test this ``decay-resistant quality'' mechanism solely as the frozen score
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{ope/be})+\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{ocf/me})
  +\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{safety})\\
  &\quad+\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{asset\ turnover})
  -\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{accruals})\\
  &\quad-\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{net\ equity\ issuance})
  -\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{debt\ growth}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Its annualized six-factor alpha is \USBestAlphaPct{}, but the simultaneous lower bound is only \USBestSimultaneousLowerPct{}. Economically, this is a profitability, cash-flow quality, safety, low-accrual, low-issuance, and low-debt-growth composite. The result may support nonlinear quality screening; it is not evidence that the AlphaAgent software generated the tested portfolio or discovered a new anomaly.

The broad control sharpens the interpretation. Over its shorter rolling evaluation window, the same AlphaAgent proxy is the only nominal positive: alpha \BroadBestAlphaPct{}, raw $p=\BroadBestRawP$. It does not survive Holm ($p=\BroadBestHolmP$) or maximum-$|t|$ inference ($p=\BroadBestMaxTP$), and its simultaneous lower bound is \BroadBestSimultaneousLowerPct{}. No other broad-JKP candidate is nominally positive. Thus there are \emph{no} statistically defensible U.S. strategies ``on top of JKP132'' in this analysis. The three classic composites previously flagged by the 134-regressor same-sample fit---Greenblatt, a multi-guru ensemble, and AlphaCrafter---are retained as a superseded exploratory diagnostic, not counted as discoveries.

The gross cross-sectional median alpha is \USMedianGrossAlphaPct{}, compared with \USMedianAlphaPct{} at \bps{10}. \subsection{Which strategies appear positive outside the United States?}

The international extension is the source of the superficially surprising ``many significant alphas.'' It is not a U.S. JKP132 result. It is a six-factor result conditional on the 27 of 62 paths that remain solvent across all six countries. Among those 27, 11 survive Holm and 12 survive maximum-$|t|$ inference; four have simultaneous lower bounds above two percentage points. The other 35 strategies generate 40 limited-liability events and have no alpha estimate. France accounts for 31 events, and 25 of all events occur in May 2022 or November 2024.

Table~\ref{tab:intlsurvivors} names every Holm survivor. The list is economically revealing: value, profitability, quality, momentum, cash, low leverage, low beta, and low volatility dominate. Four materially confirmed rows are FactorMAD, the Graham proxy, AI-Trader, and FAMA. These are returns to familiar composite screens in a secondary geography, not evidence that native agents generated novel signals. Moreover, only \SameWindowSignAgreementCount{} jointly estimable proxies share the same sign between the U.S. and international extension, and the common-calendar alpha-rank correlation is \SameWindowUSGSevenSpearman{}.

The conditional foreign result does not overturn the U.S. conclusion. It instead adds two boundary conditions. First, familiar factor combinations can be profitable in less-saturated or differently composed markets even when they add little in the United States. Second, an attractive alpha table can coexist with severe path failure: selecting only 27 survivors would hide more than half the planned family. The secondary extension therefore informs geography and implementation risk, not agentic novelty.

\section{Scientific Value, Limitations, and Implications}

The paper's principal scientific value is to turn an ambiguous reproducibility debate into an alpha-adjudication design. ``No runnable repository'' is not treated as proof of no alpha. Instead, it triggers a lower-fidelity but still substantive test. This makes the negative result harder to dismiss: direct public code supplies no benchmark-beating path, and favorable reconstructions of the missing systems yield little robust U.S. alpha after costs and search-wide inference.

The second value is explanatory. The few positive rows are not mysterious agent outputs; their formulas expose the underlying economics. The U.S. survivor is a quality and conservative-financing composite. The international survivors are recognizable value--profitability--momentum--low-risk screens. This distinction matters for AI research. A system can be useful for automating hypothesis formation, code generation, redundancy checks, or research documentation without having discovered a new priced anomaly. Demonstrating AI-generated alpha requires an ablation or provenance design that separates the agent's contribution from the return of its input factor library.

The third value is methodological. The broad-JKP audit shows why benchmark dimension and time alignment are not implementation details. An unregularized 134-regressor fit on 270 observations can manufacture impressive residual $t$-statistics; a factor panel shifted by one month can manufacture even larger ones. Rolling past-trained slopes, an unpenalized low-dimensional core, familywise inference, and a hard market-alignment check convert that fragile analysis into a defensible diagnostic with zero discoveries.

The evidence has important limits. First, the census is a dated public snapshot and cannot guarantee closure in a rapidly changing preprint literature. Second, author contact was prohibited, so the artifact results measure public reproducibility rather than private willingness to share. Third, all 62 U.S. proxies are retrospective: their ingredients were developed or inspected on the U.S. panel, and the broad-JKP diagnostic was specified after primary outcomes. The results are therefore an adversarial audit of known claims, not prospective strategy discovery. Fourth, every proxy omits some combination of language generation, memory, retrieval, tool use, timing, and endogenous execution; proxy failure does not prove native-agent failure, while proxy success cannot be attributed to an agent. Fifth, the international extension required disclosed post-outcome correctness amendments and loses 35 paths to limited liability. Finally, monthly top-1,000 portfolios and linear costs omit nonlinear impact, borrow availability, financing, capacity, and the institutional details of intraday, derivative, crypto, and market-making systems.

These limits point to a concrete release standard. A financial-agent alpha claim should preserve revision-pinned code, an environment manifest, immutable dated signal or position hashes, exact universe and timing rules, cost conventions, baseline returns, model and prompt versions, random seeds, search breadth, and failed-candidate counts. Proprietary model weights and licensed row-level data need not be redistributed for these evidence objects to exist. With them, native replication can replace mechanism reconstruction; without them, the economic content can still be tested, but the agentic contribution remains unidentified.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and responsible use.}
All headline values are generated from hash-locked registries, protocol files, result manifests, and deterministic builders. Synthetic tests cover timing, ranks, long--short weights, drift, costs, limited liability, HAC inference, multiplicity, and paired resampling. Restricted security-level market data are not redistributed; aggregate results and input hashes preserve provenance. No orders were transmitted, and the results are retrospective research evidence rather than investment advice.

\paragraph{AI-assistance disclosure.}
Generative AI tools assisted literature organization, code drafting, diagnostic review, and prose editing. The human author remains responsible for source selection, formula mappings, protocol decisions, statistical specifications, citation verification, and every claim reported here.

\section{Conclusion}

We ask whether public financial AI systems contain the alpha they report. Most claims cannot be evaluated natively from their public artifacts, but the analysis does not stop there: we attempt public code, then construct the closest transparent version of each documented economic mechanism. The direct route yields one computable code-backed U.S. path and no six-factor beater. The reconstruction route yields 62 executable U.S. portfolios, one six-factor familywise survivor, no economically confirmed effect, and no familywise survivor under a rolling broad-JKP control. The apparent foreign successes are conditional six-factor results among 27 executable paths and are dominated by familiar factor composites. The combined evidence supports a precise conclusion: public financial agents may help organize quantitative research, but the released evidence does not establish that they discover new, robust, economically material U.S. equity alpha.
