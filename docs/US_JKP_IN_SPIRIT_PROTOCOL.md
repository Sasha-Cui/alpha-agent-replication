# U.S./JKP in-spirit reconstruction protocol

## Purpose and claim boundary

The strict headline-transfer study is complete and remains frozen in
`paper_runs/us_jkp_headline`. It asked whether public paper/source materials
identified a defensible executable headline strategy without inventing the
central method. That study produced 17 common-benchmark paths and 52 honest
non-evaluable closures.

The September 4, 2026 follow-up asks a different question: **what happens when we
instantiate each paper's central idea as well as the available evidence permits,
using transparent researcher-authored substitutions, and evaluate it on the same
monthly U.S./JKP contract?** These are in-spirit reconstructions. They are neither
native replications nor tests that can falsify the papers' original empirical
claims.

Carry the 17 existing common evaluations into the new comparison without
rerunning or relabelling them. Discard the seven structural mismatches M002,
M015, M017, M031, M035, M045, and M058. Reconstruct the remaining 45 cases,
including the five for which only historical outputs were previously available.

## What an in-spirit reconstruction must preserve

Before observing the new JKP result, pin a single recipe containing:

1. Primary-source anchors for the paper's central inputs, transformation or
   reasoning mechanism, and score/action aggregation.
2. A short explanation of why this mechanism, rather than a convenient generic
   factor, represents the paper's headline contribution.
3. Three explicit lists: `preserved_elements`, `approximated_elements`, and
   `invented_elements`.
4. A deterministic score or a fully chronological learning/search procedure.
5. The paper-signed direction when disclosed; otherwise a direction fixed from
   economic logic or training-only data, never from the final common result.
6. The common score-to-portfolio adapter unless native portfolio construction is
   central and can be represented without future information.

Permitted substitutions include a transparent rule-based proxy for an
unreleased LLM policy, a small reproducible symbolic search for an unreleased
factor miner, accessible JKP characteristics for unavailable vendor fields, and
simple deterministic role aggregation for a missing multi-agent runtime. A
reconstruction may deliberately be modest; fidelity of mechanism matters more
than architectural spectacle.

## Guardrails

- Use one primary reconstruction per paper. Do not screen variants on the common
  result and report the winner.
- Never use future-return availability, published future-selected holdings, or
  historical recommendation outputs as formation-time labels.
- If search, training, memory, or adaptation is central, restrict all fitting and
  model selection to the rolling past. Record the warm-up and update schedule.
- Do not silently import another paper's final factor. Common primitives may be
  reused only when their role in the reconstruction is disclosed.
- Label every new result `in_spirit_reconstruction`. Report its distance from the
  original system and retain the strict verdict alongside it.
- Preserve the frozen 305-month calendar, top-1,000 formation universe,
  value-weighted long/short deciles, missing-return policies, 10 bp primary cost,
  and rolling JKP attribution from `us_jkp_headline_v1`.
- A negative path, weak Sharpe ratio, or insignificant JKP excess completes the
  reconstruction. It is not permission to retune.

## Completion and sequencing

One milestone is active at a time. A reconstructed milestone requires a frozen
recipe, executable implementation, focused tests, the six common cost/missingness
paths, monthly returns, attribution residuals, hash-pinned run manifest, and an
honest verdict. After every ten newly completed reconstructions, run both full
supported-Python validation gates, push once, and monitor exactly one CI run.

At completion, the new synthesis must separate carried strict adaptations,
researcher-authored in-spirit reconstructions, and structural discards. It must
not merge their fidelity labels or claim that common-benchmark performance
reproduces the papers' native results.
